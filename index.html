Task 8: banking mobile app

Ledger

Having a balance is not about being financially sound but that is also why banking applications seem ineffective whenever you require them. Having eighty four thousand shillings in your account means nothing if your rent, loan payment, and internet bill come through before the paycheck arrives. The assignment requires financial stability so this app shows the figure which is the most accurate.

Safe to spend

The primary figure displayed is the balance minus any previous amounts that have been allocated before the next source of income, then converted into a daily figure. However, this balance is featured directly below, using smaller text, as well as in a bar indicating the total of committed funds versus those available for use, plus a clickable list showing where the money is going. There are no hidden variables, every step is transparent, and you can confirm the calculations.

Transfers show the consequence first

Before confirming, the application informs you about how the payment is going to affect your spendable money and not just your balance. For example, if you send 12000, it says you are going to have 17220 before 5th October. If your transfer means going over your limit, this screen clearly indicates it in red, but you still go ahead because it is the customer’s money

Trust is built from small refusals

  • Spending is not red. Money going out is ordinary and shown in plain ink. Red appears only for a genuine problem, so when it does appear it means something.
  • Pending is labelled as pending. A transaction that has not settled is marked, and it is already deducted from safe to spend, because that is the honest position.
  • The recipient name is verified and shown before you send. The single most common way people lose money is paying the right amount to the wrong number.
  • Fees are stated on the amount screen, not on the receipt.
  • Balances can be hidden with one tap, for when you are on a matatu.

The rule: blue is the only pressable colour, green only ever means money arriving, and red is reserved for a real problem such as going below zero or a frozen card. Normal spending is black text on white, because a grocery shop is not an error.

Type

  • IBM Plex Sans throughout, with tabular figures on every amount so columns of numbers align and the total does not jitter as digits change.
  • IBM Plex Mono for account numbers, references and transaction ids, where character by character reading matters and a zero must not look like an O.
  • Scale: 46 for the amount pad, 40 for safe to spend, 36 on a receipt, then 22, 17, 15.5, 14.5, 13, 12, 11.5.
